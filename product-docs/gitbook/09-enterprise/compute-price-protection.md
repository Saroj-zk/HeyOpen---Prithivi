# Compute cost protection for enterprises

The Hey Open Enterprise page describes an approach intended to make enterprise compute costs more predictable. It combines sourcing compute capacity with financial protection against certain price movements.

## The business problem

AI inference and GPU rental costs can vary as capacity, demand, and provider pricing change. Enterprises with long-term commitments may prefer a contracted rate or predictable exposure instead of bearing every market change.

## Conceptual structure

1. **Source capacity:** Arrange the compute needed for a business workload.
2. **Protect exposure:** Use an agreed financial or contractual mechanism to limit specified price risk.
3. **Deliver service:** Quote and provide an agreed compute rate for the covered workload.
4. **Monitor effectiveness:** Reconcile realized service costs, protection costs, usage, and coverage conditions.

The website includes a simulated comparison using GPU-hour costs and an illustrative option strike and premium. Those values are examples, not current quotes, guaranteed savings, or proof that all demand can be hedged.

## Questions procurement should ask

What index or price is referenced? Which compute types and locations are covered? How long does protection last? What is excluded? How are outages, capacity shortages, usage above commitment, and contract changes handled? What counterparty and settlement risks apply?

## Caveat

Hedges do not guarantee margins, availability, or the ability to deliver an agreed service under every market condition. Financial products and compute commitments require separate contractual review.
